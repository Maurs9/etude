      Why this works

      The method behind Étude

      Étude isn’t a quiz — it’s built on what cognitive science actually says about learning a skill. Six ideas do the heavy lifting. None of them involve studying harder; they involve studying smarter.

      01

      Spaced repetition

      You remember something best when you review it right as you’re about to forget it. Each successful recall lets the next review wait a little longer.

      In Étude: every concept carries its own due-date. Today shows what’s due; the schedule stretches as you succeed and snaps back when you slip.
      02

      Retrieval practice

      Pulling an answer out of memory strengthens it far more than putting it back in by re-reading. This is the “testing effect.”

      In Étude: you always produce the answer — play the interval, build the chord, name what you hear — never just look at a chart.
      03

      Interleaving

      Mixing different problem types in one sitting feels harder than doing them in blocks — and that’s exactly why it builds more durable, flexible memory.

      In Étude: a session shuffles intervals, chords, scales and progressions together, never two of a kind back to back.
      04

      Desirable difficulty

      A little struggle is the signal that learning is happening. Effortless review barely moves the needle.

      In Étude: once you can play a concept, it graduates to by ear — and the key changes every time, so you learn the idea, not one fingering.
      05

      Immediate feedback & elaboration

      Fast correction stops errors from sticking, and a short “why” ties the new fact to things you already know.

      In Étude: every answer is followed by the formula, a familiar-song hook, and how the sound feels — so it lands in context.
      06

      Little and often

      The spacing effect: the same total practice spread across days beats one long cram. Short, frequent sessions win.

      In Étude: a daily goal and streak keep it to a five-minute habit, and tomorrow’s reviews are always waiting.

      “We are what we repeatedly do.” The hard part of theory isn’t understanding it once — it’s remembering it under your fingers, months later. That’s the part Étude is for.
